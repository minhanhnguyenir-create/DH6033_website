Temple of Heaven

天坛

The Temple of Heaven (simplified Chinese: 天坛; traditional Chinese: 天壇; pinyin: Tiāntán; Manchu: Abkai mukdehun) is a complex of religious buildings located in the southeastern part of central Beijing. It is a monumental structure of great cultural significance and is recognized by UNESCO as a World Heritage Site. Construction of the Temple of Heaven complex began in 1420 during the reign of the Yongle Emperor of the Ming dynasty. Serving as a center for imperial worship, it was the site where emperors of the Ming and Qing dynasties performed rituals to honor the deity of Heaven—known as *Hao Tian Shangdi* (the Supreme Deity of Heaven)—a practice that stood as one of the most important annual rituals throughout these dynasties. While the temple’s design reflects a Taoist influence, the tradition of worshipping Heaven in China dates back even further.[1] The complex is an architectural ensemble of immense cultural value.[2] The Temple of Heaven is the largest of the four major altars in Beijing. The others are the Temple of the Sun (日坛) to the east, the Temple of Earth (地坛) to the north, and the Temple of the Moon (月坛) to the west.
