Japanese Covered Bridge

Chùa Cầu

This bridge was built around the 17th century with funds contributed by Japanese merchants, which is why it is sometimes referred to as the Japanese Bridge. According to legend, the structure—which incorporates a small temple—acts like a sword plunged into the back of a mythical creature called Namazu, pinning it down and preventing it from thrashing its tail and causing earthquakes.[1] Later, a temple section was added, connected to the northern railing and extending over the middle of the bridge; consequently, locals have come to call it "Chùa Cầu" (Temple Bridge).
