Hoi An Ancient Town

Phố cổ Hội An

Hoi An Ancient Town is an old urban settlement located along the lower reaches of the Thu Bon River, in the coastal plain region near Da Nang, Vietnam—approximately 30 kilometers south of the city center. Benefiting from favorable geographical and climatic conditions, Hoi An once served as a bustling international trading port, a meeting point for merchant vessels from Japan, China, and the West throughout the 17th and 18th centuries. Prior to this period, the site also bore traces of a Champa trading port, often associated with the Maritime Silk Road. By the 19th century, as waterway navigation became less convenient, the port of Hoi An gradually declined, ceding its prominence to Da Nang, which was then being developed by the French. Fortunately, Hoi An escaped destruction during two major wars and avoided the rapid, large-scale urbanization that characterized the late 20th century. Beginning in the 1980s, the architectural and cultural significance of the ancient town began to garner attention from scholars and travelers alike, transforming it into one of Vietnam’s most compelling tourist destinations.
