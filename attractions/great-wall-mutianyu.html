Great Wall at Mutianyu

慕田峪长城

The Great Wall of China (simplified Chinese: 万里长城; traditional Chinese: 萬里長城; pinyin: Wànlĭ Chángchéng; literally "the long wall of ten thousand *li*"), often referred to simply as the Great Wall, is a collective term for a series of fortifications stretching thousands of kilometers from east to west. Constructed from earth and stone between the 5th century BC and the 16th century AD, it was built to protect China against incursions by the Xiongnu, Mongols, Turkic peoples, and other nomadic tribes originating from regions now known as Mongolia and Manchuria. Some sections date back as far as the 5th century BC; later, Qin Shi Huang—the first emperor of China—ordered these segments to be connected and expanded between 220 BC and 200 BC, though few traces of these early structures remain today. The well-known, frequently visited portions of the Great Wall were primarily constructed during the Ming Dynasty (1368–1644).
